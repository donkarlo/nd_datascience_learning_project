\section{Scoring rule}
Let \(m=\operatorname{median}_i x_i\) and let \(\mathrm{MAD}=\operatorname{median}_i |x_i-m|\) be the unscaled median absolute deviation. The score is
\[
z_i^* = 0.67449\frac{x_i-m}{\mathrm{MAD}}.
\]
A common heuristic flags \(|z_i^*|>3.5\). The constant makes the scale comparable to a standard z-score for normally distributed data.

\section{Example}
For a reference median of \(20\) and a MAD of \(2\), a value of \(32\) has \(z^*\approx 4.05\) and is flagged.

\section{Practical considerations}
Median-based estimates resist a small fraction of extreme values. If the MAD is zero, the score is undefined. Check for ties or quantization and use a meaningful tolerance or another scale estimate. For skewed data, calibrate the two tails separately if necessary.
